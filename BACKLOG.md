# Product Backlog

### PBI-001. Establish the Meeting Summarizer vision

Create and agree a clear vision for a local-first macOS and iOS meeting summarizer. The vision must define the intended user value, the offline and privacy boundary, the evidence-based meeting record, and the key capabilities that make the product distinct.

Test: Review confirms that the vision is internally consistent, understandable, and accepted by the Product Owner.

### PBI-010. Refine the project roadmap from the vision

Turn the generic RUP lifecycle roadmap into a project-specific roadmap after the vision is agreed. The roadmap must sequence the meaningful work for Meeting Summarizer and remain aligned with the vision, while retaining room for iterative refinement.

Test: Review confirms that the roadmap is traceable to the agreed vision, has an appropriate sequence, and is accepted by the Product Owner.

### PBI-002. Define internal meeting use cases

Define the representative internal meeting scenarios the product must support, including in-person recording, online meeting capture where permitted, shared-screen evidence, participant attribution, and real-time mention or subject alerts. The scenarios must identify the user goal, expected outcome, constraints, and evidence needed to judge the result.

Test: Review confirms that the scenarios cover the intended internal use and are consistent with the accepted vision.

### PBI-003. Establish product requirements

Define the functional and non-functional requirements derived from the approved vision and internal meeting scenarios. Requirements must state the offline boundary, privacy and permission expectations, source traceability, quality expectations, and relevant platform constraints without prescribing implementation.

Test: Review confirms that each essential requirement is clear, testable, and traceable to a vision outcome or use case.

### PBI-004. Establish the initial Product Backlog

Create and prioritize an initial Product Backlog from the requirements. Items must describe Product Owner-level outcomes, explain their value, and define an acceptance signal; they must not prescribe architecture or implementation.

Test: Review confirms that backlog items are ordered, outcome-oriented, and sufficient to plan the MVP.

### PBI-005. Define the MVP scope and acceptance criteria

Select the smallest valuable set of backlog items for the first macOS release and define the acceptance criteria for that scope. Explicitly identify deferred capabilities so the MVP boundary remains controlled.

Test: Review confirms that the MVP has a coherent user outcome, measurable acceptance criteria, and explicit exclusions.

### PBI-006. Identify constraints and risks

Identify legal, privacy, platform, model, performance, usability, and delivery risks that could affect the MVP. Record assumptions, likely impact, and the evidence or decision needed to reduce each significant risk.

Test: Review confirms that the highest risks have an owner, mitigation approach, and planned point of review.

### PBI-007. Establish the candidate architecture

Define a candidate architecture that can meet the MVP requirements while preserving local-only processing and source traceability. Identify the main responsibilities, boundaries, data flow, and technology assumptions that require validation.

Test: Review confirms that the candidate architecture addresses every essential MVP requirement and known major risk.

### PBI-008. Review the Inception baseline

Review the vision, use cases, requirements, backlog, MVP scope, risks, and candidate architecture as one coherent Inception baseline. Resolve contradictions, missing traceability, or scope conflicts before entering Elaboration.

Test: Review confirms that the Inception baseline is internally consistent and ready for architecture validation.

### PBI-009. Validate the vision against the market

Compare the Inception baseline with relevant meeting products to test whether its proposed differentiation remains meaningful. Use the findings to strengthen the vision or requirements where necessary, without copying competitor roadmaps.

Test: Review confirms that market findings either support the vision or result in explicit, accepted refinements.
